The Gas cooling effect is a central part for baryonic matter to form disks in centre of halos (see [[Galaxy Halo Connection]].)

Gas cooling happens in different channels because of different phases.
1. *Hot Gas*: ($T>10^7K$) happens in massive halos, hot gas is fully ionized and can cool through **Thermal bremsstrahlung**. At high redshift, the **Inverse Compton scattering** with CMB photons (i.e. [[Thermal SZ]]) is also efficient cooling.
2. *Warm Gas*: ($10^4<T<10^6K$), the gas is Partially Inoized, and thus the cooling happens through atomic excitation and de-excitations. The chemical composition affects the cooling greatly.
3. *Cool Gas*: ($T<10^4K$), the cool gas suppress all processes above. Cooling is still possible for collisional of heavy elements.

The Cooling Channels dependent on 2-particle collisions (**Except** Inverse Compton) is more efficient in higher density regions.

If cooling is *very effective*, the gas will accrete onto a central galaxy. If the cooling is *ineffective*, the gas settles into hydrostatic equillibrium with halo.

If the cooling is effective and there is sufficient angular momentum within the initial halo, the Gas will settle onto a central disk galaxy.
