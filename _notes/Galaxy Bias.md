The galaxy bias is introduced as the most simple galaxy-DM connection, which assumes:
$$
\delta_g=b\delta_c
$$
At large scales in [[Galaxy Clutering]], this relation is fitting well.

$$
\delta_g = \frac{N_g-\bar{N_g}}{\bar{N}_g}
$$
The galaxy bias should be dependent on:
- Redshift ,
- Luminosity and color ,
- Scale k.

The galaxy clustering 2-pt function is:
$$
\langle \delta_g \delta_g\rangle = b_g^2\langle\delta_c\delta_c\rangle=b_g^2P(k)
$$
where $P(k)$ is the Dark matter [[Matter Power Spectrum]].

The galaxy bias depends on the galaxy color:
$$
b_{red}>b_{blue}
$$
This means observation is suggesting red galaxies gives more clustering than blue galaxies.
