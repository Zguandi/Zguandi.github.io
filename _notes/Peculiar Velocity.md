# Real space and redshift space
Observed [[Redshift]] is subject to the line of sight velocity of the object and they are degenderate (generally we can't tell apart). $z = z_{Hubble}+\frac{v_{\parallel}}{c}$. The measurement of each object only gives its [[apparent velocity]] and its redshift for very high $z$ objects and we can do statistics in [[Spectrosopy]] surveys to probe the peculiar velocity.

# Linear regime
From [[Large Scale Velocities]], we know that Particles flow towards overdensities (low $\Phi$) and away from underdensities (high $\Phi$).

# Nonlinear regime
At $k<k_{NL}$ DM virialize into [[DM halo]]s and gain Line-of-sight velocity dispersion. This will cause the galaxy population to have an extended distribution in the redshift direction.

# redshift space statstics

In redsfhit space, the distribution is *distorted*, see [[Redshift space distortion]] for details.

In redshift space, the [[Matter Power Spectrum]] is also distorted, corrected by a factor dependedt on the $\mu_k = \hat{k} \cdot \hat{z}$ factor.

$$
\delta_s(\vec{k}) = [1+\beta\mu_\vec{k}^2]\delta(\vec{k})
$$
And the power spectrum:
$$
P_s(\vec{k})=P(k)[1+\beta \mu_\vec{k}^2]^2
$$

This offers another way to probe the [[Linear Growth Factor|logaritmic growth factor]]. becuse $\beta = \frac{f}{b}$. (b is the [[Galaxy Bias]]).
