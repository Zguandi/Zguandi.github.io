# Radiation bound transition
Another important equillibrium in galaxies is the line emmission and line absorption.

The line absorption strength (in terms of energy) for a population of atoms with energy levels $m,n$:
$$
k_\nu ^\ell=\frac{1}{4\pi}h\nu_{nm}(N_{m}B_{mn}-N_nB_{nm})=\frac{1}{4\pi}h\nu_{nm}N_mB_{mn}(1-\frac{g_m}{g_n}\frac{N_n}{N_m})
$$
Where the **Einstein B-coefficients** have relations: $g_m B_{mn}=g_n B{nm}$.

The Spontaneous Decay of states are described by the $A_{mn}$ factors, which has a very wide range difference.

# Line Emission

At thermal Equillibrium, 
$$
\frac{N_2}{N_1}=\frac{g_2}{g_1}\frac{e^{-E_{21}/kT}}{\underbrace{A_{21}}_{Spawt}/\underbrace{N_e Q_{21}}_{coll}+1}
$$
There are two cases:
1. **Low Density**: $N_e Q_{21}\ll A_{21}$, the particle distribution is entirely determined by collision production $\frac{N_2}{N_1}=\frac{g_2}{g_1}\frac{N_e Q_{21} }{A_{21}}e^{-E_{21}/kT}$ 
2. **High Density**: $N_eQ_{21}\gg A_{21}$, the particle is in thermal coupling with the surrounding, $\frac{N_2}{N_1}=\frac{g_2}{g_1}e^{-E_{21}/kT}$.
3. **Critical Density**: $N_eQ_{12}=A_{21}$.
