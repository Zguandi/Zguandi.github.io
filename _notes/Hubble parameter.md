# Hubble Parameter
Expansion rate of the universe, defined as:
$$
H(t)=\frac{1}{a(t)}\frac{da(t)}{dt}
$$
# Hubble constant
The *Hubble parameter* evaluated at present time.

$H=\frac{1}{a(t)}\frac{da(t)}{dt}$, $H_0=H(t_0)$

The present day measurement gives:
$$
H=100\times h \ km/(s\cdot Mpc)
$$
Where $h\sim 0.7$.

Note that:
- $H_0^{-1}\sim [time]\approx 9.78\times 10^9 h^{-1} yr$, which is the approximate [[Age of universe]].
- $\frac{c}{H}\sim[distance]\approx 3000 h^{-1}Mpc$  which is the radius of the [[Comoving Horizon]].
## The h factor.

Since many quantities depend on the not-very-accurate value $h$.
We measure distances in 'comoving units' $Mpc/h$ so that many of the h (*Not all of them* cancel).

## The Hubble Diagram

Measurement of the radial velocity of distant galaxies (that are comoving).

$$
v = \frac{d}{dt} (d_c)=H(t)\times d_c
$$
