WIMP = Weakly interacting Massive Particles. By assumption, WIMPs have interaction strength at the level of **Weak Interaction** in the *Particle Physics Standard Model*.

## *Hot* WIMPs

*Hot* WIMPs freeze out when they are relativistic:  $\left(x_f \ll 1\right)$. Calculating the particle [[Particle Relics]] abundance gives:
$$
\Omega_{i, 0} h^2=7.6 \times 10^{-2}\left[\frac{g_{\text {ieff }}}{g_{*_s}\left(x_f\right)}\right] \frac{m_i}{1 \mathrm{eV}}
$$
For these particles to contribute significantly to the energy density today (e.g., bulk of DM), we need:

$$
\Omega_{i, 0} \approx 0.3 \quad \Rightarrow \quad m_i \sim 30 \mathrm{eV}
$$
This model for Dark matter is called HDM, but it is ruled out by the [[Large Scale Structure]] Measurments.
## *Cold* WIMPs

*Cold* WIMPS: $\left(x_f \gg 1\right)$, they freeze out at non-relativistic temperature
Here:
$$
\Omega_{i, 0} h^2 \approx\left\{\begin{array}{cl}
1.8\left(\frac{m_i}{1 \mathrm{GeV}}\right)^{-2}\left[1+0.17 \ln \frac{m_i}{\mathrm{GeV}}\right] & \text { if } m_i \lesssim 100 \mathrm{GeV} \\
\left(\frac{m_i}{3 \mathrm{TeV}}\right)^2 & \text { if } m_i \gtrsim 100 \mathrm{GeV}
\end{array}\right.
$$

So, again:
$$
\Omega_{i, 0} \approx 0.3 \quad \Rightarrow \quad m_i\left\{\begin{array}{l}
\sim 2 \mathrm{GeV} \\
\sim 2 \mathrm{TeV}
\end{array}\right. \text { or }
$$
This Model is a typical model of CDM ([[LCDM model]]), and in terms of [[Cosmic Microwave Background]] and [[Large Scale Structure]], it agrees pretty well. However, no *Direct Detection* has detected particles at this energy scale with a *Weak*  interaction. So it is very challenged as a candidate of CDM.
