In descending order below are the relevant processes's timescale for [[Galaxy Gas Cycle]].

1. Universe Age/ Hubble time $1/H$ any timescale larger than this is less meaningful.
2. Dynamical time: time to orbit across steady state dynamical system.
3. Cooling time: ratio between thermal energy and [[Gas Cooling]].
4. Star formation time: Ratio of cold gas mass and [[Star formation rate]].
5. Chemical enrichment timescale: Timescale of galaxy to be enriched by heavy elements from [[SNIa|Supernova]].
6. Merging time: typical timescale between [[Merger Tree]] branches.
7. Dynamical Friction Time: Time for a satellite in the halo to lose angular momentum and fall into centre.

Examples of the analysis:
1. A satellite galaxy with mass $\ll$ parent halo has Dynamical friction time > Hubble time, and this suggests that galaxys could have stable satellites.
2. If cooling time > dynamical time, the hot gas will be in mostly hydrostatic equillibrium. In the opposite case, gasses cool quickly and settle down onto centre disk.
3. If Star formation time $\simeq$ dynamical time, gas will convert into star during collapse, and will typically lead to an elliptical galaxy. On the contrary, if Star formaion time > dynamical time, gas will first settle down into a disk and form stars afterwards on the disk, giving disk galaxies. (*Angular momentum also plays a role*)
4. If Chemical evolution time > the star formation time, little enrichment will occur during star formation and stars will end up with the same initial metallicity.
