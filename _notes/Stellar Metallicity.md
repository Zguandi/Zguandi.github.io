Z: mass fraction of all elements heavier than He
Y: mass fraction of He
X: mass fraction of H

## Sun
For our sun, $X=0.7,Y=0.7,Z=0.02$

Younger stars have more metal from corpses of previous stars, and older stars have less metallicty.

> [!QUESTION] Does existence of a certain metal line indicate higher metallicity?
> Not Exactly! Existence of certain lines only tell information about [[Spectral Types]], only the [[Integrated Strength]] tells about the Metallicity.

## Alternative notation

$$
[Fe/H]=\log(Fe/H)_{star}-\log(Fe/H)_{sun}
$$
Sometimes its not even the sun, but the stellar average.

## How to measure metallicity

- **Integrated Strength** of the spectral line in High-Resolution [[Spectrosopy]].
- Overall Information (line opacity and shift in colors)

# Chemical Evolution of Metallicity

The present day Milky way is composed by:
- [[Globular clusters]]: Contains Metal poor stars $[Fe/H]<-1$, $t_{age}>10\sim13 Gyr$. and Contains mainly Pop II stars (see [[Stellar Populations]]). Dynamics include infalling gas and objects.
- Galactic [[Galactic Bulge]]: A mix of metal rich/metal poor stars. This implies the stars have different formation scenarios. And metallicity is enriched by SN explosions.
- Galactic [[Galactic Disk]]: [[Metallicity Gradient in Milky Way]].
