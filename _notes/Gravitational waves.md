In June 1916, Einstein wrote a paper that predicts gravitational waves. At that time this was thoughht to be impossible to detect.

Gravitational waves are linearized perturbation of the [[Einstein's Equation]], see [[Einstein'e Equation perturbation|Metric Gauge Transfroms]]. The gravitational wave is acting line a spin-2 massless particle [graviton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graviton).

Gravitational waves, like EM waves, travels at $c$, and also experiences [[Lensing]] effects. However there are fundamental differences:
- very weak $\delta L/ L = 10^{-21}\sim 10^{-24}$ for a interfererometer.
- negligible scattering with matter **This makes it a perfect messenger**
- The measurement, direct measure wave amplitudes (unlike in EM waves, measurement is in *Intensity*, or amplitude squared) and decays with $1/r$ instead of $1/r^2$.

> [!info] Hulse-Taylor Binary pulsars
> Discovered in 1974, the measurement of orbital decay PSR 1913+16 binary pulsar system gives a perfect fit for the energy loss by the theorized gravitational wave.
