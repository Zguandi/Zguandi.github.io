Usually observation of stellar populations are subject to:
- Magnitude Limit
- Volume Limit

Thus we usually only observe *Integrated counts to a brightness limit for given spectal type*.
$$
N(m_{low},S)=\int_{-\infty}^{m_{low}}A^c(m,S)dm
$$
## Assume Homogeneous Distribution

From [[The Cosmological Principle]], things are distributed evenly larger than $k_{NL}^{-1}$.
$$
N(r_{lim})=\Omega D_0\int_0^{r_{lim}} r^2dr = \frac{\Omega D_0}{3}r_{lim}^3
$$
Then we can have a relation of $N(m)$ as a function of the apparent [[Magnitudes]]. 
$$
\log N(m)=0.6\log m_{lim}+C(\Omega,D_0)
$$
Which translates to ~increase by factor of 4 per magnitude difference.

## In reality

Things are more complicated in reality and there is extinction and inhomogeneous distribution of stars.
